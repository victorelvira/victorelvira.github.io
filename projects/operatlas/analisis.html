Trends

Reading the corpus as a whole: which voices each kind of opera leans on, when the genres rose and fell, and how a single composer's output changed across a career. Everything below is computed live from the fields in the fichas — nothing pre-baked.

Vocal profile by category

Share of each voice type among the named roles of every opera in a category. A quick way to compare, say, the tenor-and-soprano world of verismo with the lower-voiced weight of French grand opéra. Click a category to see its operas.

When the genres rose and fell

Premieres per decade, by category (darker = more). The diagonal drift from opera seria and tragédie lyrique toward opera buffa, grand opéra and verismo is the shape of operatic history.

A composer's arc

Every opera we hold for one composer, by premiere year; bar length = median running time, colour = category. Pick Verdi to watch bel-canto brevity grow into the long through-composed dramas.

Tonality as the opera unfolds — from real scores

The dream was a tonal map of every opera; almost none survives as a complete analysable score, so this is honest about where each line comes from (see the note on the music). Complete operas, all voices: Weber's Der Freischütz (scholarly MEI edition), Wagner's whole Ring, Peri's L'Euridice (with expert harmonic analysis), and six Moniuszko operas number by number. Famous numbers of the great Italian/French repertoire — Traviata, Tosca, Bohème, Carmen… — the key of each iconic aria or overture. Hue = key (circle of fifths); brighter = major. Hover any block.

Categories from Wikidata genre; voices from Wikipedia role tables; years from Wikidata. Tonality from CC-BY symbolic scores (NIFC humdrum-polish-scores) analysed with music21. Operas without a stated category, roles or score are omitted from the relevant chart, so counts are of what is known, not of everything.
